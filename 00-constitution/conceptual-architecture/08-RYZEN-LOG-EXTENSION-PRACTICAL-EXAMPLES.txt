Purpose
This log clarifies the long-term purpose of ARC creation, ARC specialization, ecosystem convergence, and the relationship between Ryzen, Hermes, and future ARCs.
This clarification exists to prevent uncontrolled ARC proliferation, architectural fragmentation, specialization dilution, and loss of ecosystem coherence.
________________

Foundational Principle
The objective of Ryzen is not to create as many ARCs as possible.
The objective of Ryzen is to create and maintain a small number of highly capable, deeply specialized, operationally mature ARCs whose accumulated experience can converge into Ryzen over time.
Success is measured by:
* Depth of learning
* Quality of specialization
* Operational maturity
* Cross-domain intelligence
* Controlled convergence
Not by ARC quantity.
________________

ARC Creation Doctrine
New ARCs must never be created merely because they can be created.
ARC creation requires validation.
Valid creation triggers include:
1. Operational Necessity
Existing ARCs cannot adequately absorb a required specialization.
2. Strategic Opportunity
A significant opportunity exists that requires a dedicated intelligence universe.
3. Knowledge Gap
Ryzen identifies a missing reality domain that is strategically important.
4. Long-Term Specialization Need
A domain requires sustained learning that cannot reasonably remain inside an existing ARC.

Future ARC Necessity Test
A useful future rule could be:
A new ARC should only exist if it represents a genuinely distinct civilization domain that cannot naturally live inside an existing ARC.
For example:
Finance ARC
* How value moves.
Knowledge ARC
* How information moves.
Energy ARC
* How power/resources move.
These would qualify because they represent different fundamental flows.
Whereas:
* Restaurant ARC
* Real Estate ARC
* Construction ARC
would likely be domains inside existing civilization flows rather than deserving sovereign ARC status.
________________

What's interesting is that without explicitly planning it this way, your ecosystem is starting to resemble a map of the major flows that drive human civilization:
* Mind
* Attention
* Desire
* Organization
* Human Movement
* Goods Movement
That's a much deeper framing than simply having several software products, and it provides a useful long-term filter for deciding whether future ARCs are truly necessary or just distractions.
