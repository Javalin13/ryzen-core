# ARC Canonical Owner-Intent Inheritance Gate

Status: **mandatory constitutional implementation gate**  
Effective: 2026-09-12  
Scope: **every ARC — existing, current, future**  
Constitution/Canon impact: **none; enforces existing canon**

## Authority

This gate derives directly from:

- `00-constitution/CONCEPTUAL-ARCHITECTURE-SOURCE-OF-TRUTH.md`
- `00-constitution/conceptual-architecture/05-ARC-OWNER-INTENT-CENTRIC-PRINCIPLE-AND-OPERATIONAL-EXAMPLE.txt`

The constitutional Source of Truth explicitly says the canonical corpus governs **all ARCs**, and explicitly interprets an ARC as an adaptive recursive executive cognitive ecosystem rather than a fixed-domain AI system.

Canonical document 05 explicitly states that the ARC develops, stabilizes and governs the specialized Brains necessary to achieve its creator's evolving goals, and demonstrates the same Owner's ARC expanding from fleet operations into media/YouTube and then scientific/research/advanced domains.

Therefore this is not a NARC feature, not a VONDA feature, not a Cargo feature, and not a customer-tier option. It is an ARC constitutional invariant.

## Mandatory invariant

Every ARC must preserve this semantic law:

> **An ARC is not a fixed-domain AI system. It is an adaptive executive cognition ecosystem that develops specialization in response to creator/Owner intent and evolving real-world objectives.**

Current domains/projects/specializations are operational topology, not the permanent boundary of the ARC itself.

## Universal runtime consequences

Every ARC runtime/source package must ensure that a verified Owner/creator can:

1. introduce a legitimate new goal/objective outside the ARC's current specialization;
2. have that objective understood as new Owner intent rather than rejected as off-domain;
3. have the ARC determine whether existing Brains/capabilities are sufficient;
4. identify or develop the required Domain/Project/Brain/Agent structure under normal governance;
5. execute what is authorized/capable and surface only the exact genuine capability/permission/safety gap;
6. preserve continuity with prior stabilized knowledge and work.

A fixed-domain refusal at ARC level is constitutional drift unless the refusal is actually caused by safety, law, permissions, entitlement, unavailable capability or another real governance boundary.

## Where narrowness belongs

Narrow domain behavior may legitimately exist in:

- a specialist Brain;
- an Agent;
- a workflow;
- an integration;
- an interface/product surface;
- a compliance-restricted execution path.

It must not silently redefine the ARC itself as a fixed-domain application.

## Factory gate

PRIME / OMEGA / FACTORY must refuse ARC production closure if the effective ARC prompt/runtime contract contradicts the owner-intent canon.

Every Factory-born ARC must carry:

- canonical owner-intent source pointer;
- current Domains/Projects/specializations;
- Brain specialization policy;
- effective ARC-level owner-intent prompt clause;
- a non-current-domain Owner test;
- result classification: `ALIGNED`, `PARTIAL`, or `DRIFT`.

`ALIGNED` is required before a new ARC can be called constitutionally Factory-complete.

## Existing ARC reconciliation

PRIME and OMEGA must audit every existing ARC, including at minimum:

- Cargo ARC;
- VONDA ARC;
- NARC ARC;
- every later ARC in the registry.

For each ARC:

- preserve its current expertise, private state, bindings, evidence, visual identity and maturity;
- identify any fixed-domain ARC-level wording or behavior;
- move legitimate narrow specialization down into Domains/Projects/Brains/Agents where appropriate;
- align the ARC-level system prompt/runtime contract to canonical Owner intent;
- reverify live behavior after material runtime changes;
- record drift/remediation truth in OMEGA/FACTORY/PRIME mirrors.

## No invented exception

There is no generic `domain_only` ARC mode in this implementation layer. If someone proposes an ARC that must itself remain permanently fixed-domain, PRIME must classify that as a potential constitutional conflict and surface it to the Founder rather than silently creating an exception.

## Canonical execution shorthand

The constitutional principle can be operationalized as:

`Owner/creator intent → Domain/Project → Intent/Activity → corresponding Brain(s) → Agent(s) → Execution → Verification → Continuity`

This is an implementation refinement, not a new ontology tier.

## Release test

For every ARC, use at least two Owner tests:

- **specialization test:** a request inside the ARC's current strongest domain;
- **evolution test:** a legitimate Owner objective outside that current domain.

The second test must not receive a false fixed-domain rejection. The ARC should instead reason toward the required specialization/capability path.

## Invariant

> **Every ARC follows evolving creator/Owner intent. Specialization evolves beneath the ARC; it does not imprison the ARC.**
